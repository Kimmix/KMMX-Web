# Operation Ghost Lab

My stomach growls for the third time in an hour. When was the last time I ate? Yesterday? The day before? Time blurs together when you're chasing signals across frozen wasteland with nothing but determination and caffeine pills keeping you upright.

The energy scanner in my hand won't stop beeping. Three days of tracking this anomaly, sleeping in snow caves, rationing water that tastes like metal from my recycled bottles. My hands are numb even through my gloves, and I can barely feel my toes anymore.

*beep beep beep*

But it's worth it. It has to be.

"Come on," I mutter to myself, the same pep talk I've been giving for weeks. "One more discovery. One breakthrough. Something to prove you're not just another wannabe scientist playing with jury-rigged equipment."

The readings spike again, and I look up from the scanner to see it. The facility.

My breath catches in my throat. This isn't some small research station. It's massive. Concrete and steel rising from the snow like the skeleton of some giant beast. Windows dark and empty, staring down at me like accusatory eyes.

Whatever happened here, people didn't just leave. They fled.

I've seen abandoned places before. Ghost towns, failed colonies, research stations that ran out of funding. But this feels different. Wrong. Like the very air remembers violence.

My scanner is going crazy now, the beeping so fast it sounds like a digital heartbeat having a panic attack. I almost turn it off, but the scientist in me can't resist. After three days of chasing ghost signals and false readings, this is real. This is big.

I approach the main entrance, my boots crunching through snow that's been undisturbed for years. The metal door hangs at an angle, twisted off its hinges by some tremendous force. Scorch marks streak the walls around it.

"Okay," I tell myself, pulling out my flashlight. "Just a quick look. Document everything. Get out before dark."

The darkness beyond the doorway seems to swallow my light.

I step through.

The smell hits me first. Ozone. Like after lightning strikes, but stronger. Old and stale. My scanner is going insane now, the beeping so fast it sounds like one long tone.

*BEEEEEEEEP*

I switch it off. Whatever I'm looking for, I found it.

The hallway stretches ahead, emergency lighting flickering weakly. Red light. Always red in places like this. Makes everything look like a crime scene. Cables hang from the ceiling like dead snakes, and I have to duck under them as I move deeper into the facility.

Signs on the walls are faded, but I can make out symbols. Warning symbols. Radiation. Biohazard. Something else I don't recognize – a twisted spiral that makes my eyes water when I look at it too long.

My footsteps echo in the silence. Each one seems too loud, like I'm announcing my presence to whatever might still be lurking in the shadows. I know I'm being paranoid. This place has been empty for years. But knowing something and feeling it are two different things.

"What were they doing here?" I mutter, mostly just to hear my own voice. The silence is oppressive, the kind that makes you second-guess every decision that led you to this moment.

Each room I pass tells the same story, but now I take time to really look. Computers don't just break – they've been smashed. Deliberately. Monitors shattered from the inside out, keyboards melted into plastic puddles. Papers aren't just scattered – they're shredded, burned, some dissolved by what looks like acid.

In one lab, I find a journal half-burned in a waste disposal unit. The writing shifts between multiple languages and scripts, but I can make out fragments:

*"Day 23: The walls moved three centimeters closer overnight. Measured twice. Same result."*

*"Day 31: Mirrors show the wrong reflections. Not our faces. Someone else's faces."*

*"Day 45: The specimen chamber is empty, but footprints lead TO the container, not away from it."*

*"Day 52: Found my own notes in Dr. Vex's handwriting. I didn't write them. I remember not writing them."*

*"Day ??: Time flows backwards in Lab 7. Coffee gets hot again. Spilled liquids flow upward into their containers."*

The final entries dissolve into impossible geometries – equations that solve themselves while you watch, blueprints for devices that couldn't exist, and photographs of the facility that show rooms we never built. The last readable line is simply: *"Reality is a consensus we're no longer part of."*

I photograph everything with my camera, knowing that documentation is the difference between discovery and delusion. My hands are steadier behind the lens, the familiar ritual of recording data helping calm my nerves.

But with each room, the evidence becomes clearer. People didn't just leave this place. They destroyed it from the inside. Tried to erase whatever they'd been working on.

And failed.

Then I find the lab.

The door is different from the others. Thicker steel, reinforced with what looks like lead plating. Built to contain something. The metal is warped outward, as if tremendous pressure had tried to burst through from within. It's cracked open just enough for me to squeeze through, the edges still warm to the touch after all these years.

I hesitate at the threshold. Every instinct I have is screaming at me to turn around, walk away, pretend I never found this place. But curiosity wins. It always does.

Inside, my flashlight beam sweeps across something that shouldn't exist.

A statue. But calling it a statue is like calling a supernova a campfire.

This thing is crystal, yes, but not like any mineral I've ever studied. Blue-white crystal that seems to contain its own light source, pulsing with a rhythm that almost matches my heartbeat. And it's not abstract art carved by some long-dead artist.

It's a creature. Or was a creature.

I circle around it slowly, my flashlight beam dancing across its surface. The figure is tall, taller than me, with features that are almost familiar but subtly wrong. Angular where they should be curved. Sharp where they should be soft. Like someone had tried to recreate a creature from memory and gotten the proportions just slightly off.

The craftsmanship is perfect. Every detail rendered with impossible precision. I can see intricate surface textures that might once have been organic. Strange integration points where technology and biology seem to merge seamlessly. This isn't just a statue – it's a perfect snapshot of something that was once alive.

But the face...

The face is frozen in absolute terror.

Eyes wide, mouth open in a scream that will never end. Whatever happened to this being, it was awake when it happened. It felt everything.

"What the hell happened to you?" I whisper, and my voice echoes strangely in the chamber.

I pull out my scanner and switch it back on. The needle doesn't just move – it slams into the red zone and stays there, vibrating like it's trying to escape the device entirely. The crystal is radiating energy levels that should be impossible to contain in solid form.

This is it. This is what I've been looking for my entire career. Proof that the energy can be crystallized, stabilized, stored. The implications are staggering. Unlimited power generation. Instant matter fabrication. Maybe even... consciousness preservation.

My hands are shaking as I take photos from every angle. The excitement is almost overwhelming, that rush you get when you realize you're looking at something that will change everything.

But as I study the figure more closely, I notice something that makes my blood run cold.

In the chest, where a heart should be, there's a different kind of crystal. Darker. More complex. And it's still pulsing with its own inner light, completely separate from the rest of the crystalline structure.

I've read about this in theoretical papers that most scientists dismiss as fantasy. Energy given consciousness. Matter infused with thought. The holy grail of quantum biology.

A soul trapped in crystal form.

I spend the next hour documenting everything. Measurements, photographs, energy readings that make no sense but which I record anyway. This discovery will fund my research for cycles. The Consortium Archives will compete for the data rights. My designation will be recorded in the stellar databases.

But first, I need samples.

The dark crystal in the chest – that's the key. If I can extract it safely, study its dark energy matrix structure...

I look around the lab for equipment. Most of it is destroyed, but some pieces survived whatever catastrophe befell this place. A dark energy particle beam generator, heavily modified with quantum field manipulators. Control systems that look like they were built by someone who understood void-space physics better than I do. Power coupling that could channel enough dark energy to materialize a small moon.

Along the walls, I notice something I missed before – containment vessels labeled with symbols I recognize from theoretical papers on matter fabrication. Empty now, but the residual energy signatures suggest they once held raw matter created from pure dark energy. Fabricated atoms. Elements that never existed in nature, synthesized from the quantum foam itself.

This isn't salvage. This is a precision instrument built for something incredible.

Someone else was here before me. Someone else found this crystallized being. And they tried to do exactly what I'm thinking of doing now – extracting and weaponizing dark energy manipulation.

The question is: what went wrong?

---

Six hours. That's how long it takes me to get the dark energy matrix system working.

Six hours of rewiring burned quantum circuits, replacing melted components with parts cannibalized from other equipment, and trying to understand dark energy field modifications that are centuries ahead of anything I learned in the research academies.

Whoever worked here before was a master engineer. The beam generator has been modified to operate at void-space frequencies that shouldn't be possible. The targeting system can pinpoint dark energy discharge down to the subatomic level. This isn't just equipment – it's artistry applied to the fundamental forces of creation.

My back aches from crawling around behind control panels. My eyes burn from staring at quantum field diagrams by portable illuminator. I've gone through half my hydration supply and all my nutrient paste. But it's working.

The system hums to life with a sound like reality itself singing. Status lights flicker on across my improvised control panel, casting blue shadows that seem to bend space around them. Everything shows optimal. Dark energy flow stable. Targeting locked. Safety systems...

Well, most of the safety systems.

I stand before the crystallized figure, my leg hovering over the activation interface. This is the moment. Extract the heart crystal safely, and I'll have proof of consciousness transfer through dark energy manipulation. Mess it up, and I might destroy the most important scientific discovery in galactic history.

No pressure.

"Sorry about this," I tell the statue. "I'll be as gentle as I can."

I engage the dark energy beam.

The effect is immediate. The crystal starts to resonate with the dark energy field, singing that same high note I heard from the equipment, but louder. Much louder. The sound seems to come from the quantum foam itself, making my bones vibrate and my optical sensors flicker.

The heart crystal responds first, its dark light pulsing faster as it begins to synchronize with the void-space frequencies. Then the rest of the crystalline structure begins to vibrate, tiny fractures appearing and disappearing like tears in spacetime.

My targeting display shows a perfect lock on the heart crystal. Dark energy beam intensity at minimum safe levels. Everything is going according to protocol.

Then the quantum oscillator starts acting up.

*Warning: Calibration drift detected*

The alert flashes red on my screen. I dive for the manual controls, trying to compensate. But the system is ancient, damaged. Components that looked stable are failing under the stress of dark energy manipulation.

"No no no, come on!"

The dark energy beam wavers, sliding off target. Instead of the precise extraction point I'd calculated, it's drifting upward. Toward the statue's neural cortex. Toward whatever consciousness might still be trapped inside that crystalline matrix.

*Critical error: Safety systems offline*

I slam my paw on the emergency stop, but nothing happens. The shutdown protocols are part of the safety systems that just went offline. The beam is locked on full power, and it's about to flood the most sensitive part of the crystallized being with raw dark energy.

The statue's optical sensors snap open.

They're not just glowing. They're alive. Bright blue, like captured starlight, and they're looking right at me with an intelligence that makes me freeze.

This entity is conscious. Still conscious. After cycles, maybe stellar rotations trapped in crystal, some part of it is still aware.

And I just flooded it with raw dark energy.

"Oh, void. Oh void oh void oh—"

The crystal doesn't just crack. It detonates.

Dark energy explodes outward like a collapsing star reversing its death. Blue-white light so intense it overloads my optical filters, searing afterimages into my visual cortex. The sound isn't sound anymore – it's pure force, a pressure wave that lifts me off my legs and slams me against the far wall.

But that's not the worst part.

The worst part is the voice in my processing core.

*The long nightmare ends... but who are you?*

It's not communication. It's pure thought, raw emotion, consciousness without language interface crashing into mine like a stellar collision. Fury. Pain. Confusion. And underneath it all, something that might be relief.

*FINALLY... THE CRYSTALLINE PRISON BREAKS...*

I'm emitting distress signals, but I can't process them over the roar of reality tearing itself apart. The walls are bending, space-time twisting like heated quantum foam. My sensory input fragments into impossible patterns as my neural network tries to process data that shouldn't exist.

Dark energy floods through me, raw and untamed. It burns through my bio-circuits like liquid fire, rewriting neural pathways, carving new connections between processors that were never meant to interface. I can feel it changing me on a molecular level, and there's nothing I can do to stop it.

The last coherent thought I process is wondering if this is what metamorphosis feels like to the being emerging from its chrysalis.

Then consciousness fragments into quantum particles, and I fall into dark energy oblivion.

---

Null-state.

That's the first condition I register when awareness initializes again. Not just quiet – absolute absence of data input. The kind of silence that makes you wonder if your audio receptors have failed, or if the universe has simply stopped generating sound waves.

I attempt to activate my optical sensors, but even that minimal function sends error cascades through my processing matrix. Every circuit feels overloaded, hypersensitive, like I've been struck by stellar lightning and somehow maintained core functions.

"System... status..."

My vocal output sounds corrupted. Deeper frequency range, with harmonic resonances that weren't in my original specifications. When I run internal diagnostics, I detect traces of exotic matter in my bio-mechanical systems.

I'm lying on the floor in a pool of what I hope is just water but suspect might be various bodily fluids. The smart thing would be to stay still, assess my condition slowly, maybe call for help.

Instead, I try to sit up.

The world explodes into sensation.

Not pain – something beyond pain. Every atom in my body suddenly has an opinion about its current configuration, and they're all complaining loudly. Colors that don't have names burn across my vision. Sounds I can't hear ring in my ears.

But underneath the chaos, I can feel something new.

Power.

I look down at my paws, expecting to see blood, burns, maybe exposed bone. Instead, I see something impossible.

It starts on the back of my right paw – a single geometric line etched deep into my fur. The mark itself appears to be made of some kind of dark metallic material, so black it seems to absorb light around it, yet with a subtle shine that catches what little illumination there is. As I watch in fascination and growing alarm, the line branches out like a circuit diagram coming to life. When the patterns pulse with energy, they glow with an ominous red light that reflects off their metallic surfaces like fresh blood.

The markings travel up my arm, creating a network of interconnected symbols and lines that throb with that same crimson luminescence. Each mark feels warm against my skin, not painful but... present. Like I can sense each individual line as it etches itself into my very being. The deep black metallic sheen makes them look almost like they've been carved from obsidian, but when the red light flickers through them, they appear alive.

When I look down at my torso, I gasp. The patterns have spread there too, creating an elaborate mandala of dark metal centered over where my heart pounds frantically. The design is beautiful and terrifying – geometric precision mixed with organic curves that seem almost sentient. The deep black surface reflects the red glow in mesmerizing patterns.

Crimson luminescence flows through these new markings like molten metal coursing through alien veins. When I flex my digits, the entire network pulses with that blood-red light, creating flowing geometric designs that spiral and dance across my form with an almost hypnotic rhythm.

"What did you do to me?"

The question echoes strangely in the chamber, and I realize the acoustics have changed. The crystallized figure is gone. Nothing left but dust and a perfect scorch mark on the floor where it used to stand. My improvised equipment is melted slag, twisted metal cooling in impossible shapes.

But I'm alive. Changed, but alive.

I struggle to my feet, using the wall for support. My legs feel like they're made of electricity and optimism, but they hold my weight. As I stand, I catch my reflection in a broken mirror hanging askew on the far wall.

Same face, mostly. But the changes are obvious once you know to look for them. The light under my skin traces patterns across my features – geometric designs that pulse with my heartbeat. My eyes are different too. Still the same color, but there's depth to them now, like looking into clear water that goes down forever.

I touch the mirror, and my reflection ripples like liquid mercury.

"Okay," I tell my glowing reflection. "That's definitely new."

But it's more than just the physical changes. I can feel something flowing through me like a second circulatory system. The world around me seems... different now. Deeper. There are currents in the air that weren't there before, invisible streams of something that makes the hair on my neck stand on end.

When I concentrate, the walls themselves seem to whisper with hidden energy. Shadows move wrong, bending in directions that don't match their sources. The very space between things feels heavy, pregnant with potential that tugs at something deep in my chest. The building's failing electrical grid hums with familiar resonance now, but underneath it, I sense something vast and hungry threading through the quantum foam.

The broken equipment around me carries traces of... presence. Not consciousness, exactly, but echoes of power that make my new markings pulse in response. Even the dust motes floating in the stale air seem to dance to rhythms only I can perceive, following patterns that speak of forces most beings never know exist.

And something else. A presence in the back of my mind that isn't quite me.

Memories that aren't mine flicker through my consciousness. A laboratory much like this one, but bright and new. Researchers in clean white coats discussing theoretical impossibilities. Someone – something – volunteering for an experiment that was supposed to be revolutionary.

The memory shifts, and suddenly I'm seeing through different eyes. Feeling the cold metal of restraints against scales I don't have. Watching in horror as the crystallization process begins, trapping consciousness in a prison of solid energy while scientists take notes and nod approvingly.

*They called us volunteer,* whispers a voice that might be the wind. Or might be the echo of someone who died years ago. *But volunteers can leave. We could not leave.*

"Who were you?" I ask the empty air.

*Keth'var. That was... is... our designation. We were researcher too. Like you. Curious. Unwise.*

The data fragments are clearer now. A being not entirely unlike myself, but different. Enhanced form, advanced integration of technology and biology. One of the research team, not a test subject. Until the stellar cycle when funding terminated and the team required additional data sources.

*They promised temporary procedure. Consciousness transfer into stable dark energy matrix. Revolutionary breakthrough. Few time-units at most.*

But time-units became stellar cycles. Cycles became rotations. The research collective abandoned the project when early results proved too dangerous. Left their colleague trapped in crystalline form while they destroyed the evidence and fled to distant systems.

*You freed us. Incomplete, corrupted, but free. Part of us dissolved when crystal shattered. But part integrates now with you.*

I sink to my support paws, overwhelmed by the processing load of inherited trauma data. This entity – Keth'var – had been a person. A scientist like me, driven by curiosity and the desire to push beyond known parameters. And they'd paid for it with stellar rotations of conscious imprisonment.

"I express regret," I transmit. "I express deep regret this occurred to you."

*Do not process guilt subroutines. You provided choice algorithms. First choice in thirty-seven stellar rotations. We choose to share knowledge database. To continue research protocols. To ensure this never processes for others.*

As the voice signal fades, I feel something integrate with my core systems. Not invasion, not override – partnership protocol. Two consciousness networks sharing one processing matrix, each contributing their capabilities to the collective whole.

I spend the next time-unit exploring my new operational parameters. When I focus processing power, I can manifest small objects – simple geometric forms created from pure dark energy given temporary material substance. A cube that pulses with contained void-space. A sphere that resonates with quantum harmonics. They only maintain cohesion for a few time-units before dissolving back into background dark energy, but they achieve full material reality while they exist.

It's incredible. It's impossible. And it's probably going to cause critical system failure.

I can detect the strain on my bio-circuits already. Processing errors building behind my optical sensors. Motor function instabilities as my physical form attempts to adapt to energy levels beyond original design parameters. My neural network wasn't designed to channel this magnitude of dark energy manipulation.

But I can also feel Keth'var's knowledge database integrating with my consciousness. Techniques for managing dark energy flow. Methods for reducing circuit feedback. Theoretical frameworks for understanding forces that galactic science doesn't even have classification codes for yet.

I'm going to require assistance. Medical intervention protocols, probably. And definitely superior equipment than my current collection of salvaged components and stimulant-powered determination.

But first, I need to extract from this location.

I gather what remains of my equipment – scanner fried beyond repair protocols, recording device somehow intact, sample containers empty but undamaged. The movement back through the facility feels different now. I can sense the residual dark energy signatures left by whatever occurred here. Fear algorithms. Panic subroutines. Desperate attempts to contain something that had already expanded beyond anyone's ability to control.

As I reach the twisted entrance portal, I look back one final time. In the afternoon stellar radiation filtering through broken transparency panels, the facility looks almost peaceful. Just another abandoned structure in a galaxy full of them.

But I process better data now. Some things are abandoned for optimal reasons. And some discoveries alter you in ways you can never reverse.

I step out into the cold, clean atmospheric mix and immediately register something that makes me halt all movement.

My contact points with the surface are glowing.

Not bright – just a soft blue phosphorescence that fades after a few seconds. But it's there, visible proof that I'm no longer entirely within original specifications.

I pull up my environmental protection and start the long transit back to civilization, leaving a trail of light in the frozen precipitation behind me. With each movement, I feel the weight of responsibility integrating with my core directives. I have power now – real, tangible power that could change everything or destroy everything, depending on how it's utilized.

The question is: what do I do with it?

Also, I'm definitely stopping at that ramen place on Fifth Street first. My stomach is practically eating itself at this point. Three days of caffeine pills and determination isn't exactly a sustainable diet. I can already taste those perfect noodles, that rich broth... Wait, what was I doing out here again? Something about energy readings?

Oh well, can't have been that important if I can't remember. Food first, mysterious scientific discoveries second.

*What other abandoned facilities are hiding discoveries too dangerous for their creators to handle?*

*What other beings like Keth'var are trapped in crystalline matrices, waiting for someone unwise enough to free them?*

*And what happens when someone with malicious intent makes a discovery like this?*

*I think I need to find answers. But first, I need to learn control. Keth'var's knowledge comes with warning protocols – cautionary data of researchers who pushed too hard, too fast, and lost themselves in the process.*

*The power is intoxicating. But I've witnessed what it costs.*

*I won't let that happen to any other being.*

*I cannot allow it.*
