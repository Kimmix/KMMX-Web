# Operation Ghost Lab

My stomach growls for the third time in an hour. When was the last time I ate? Yesterday? The day before? Time blurs together when you're chasing signals across frozen wasteland with nothing but determination and caffeine pills keeping you upright.

The energy scanner in my paw won't stop beeping. Three days of tracking this anomaly, sleeping in snow caves, rationing water that tastes like metal from my recycled bottles. My paws are numb even through my gloves, and I can barely feel my toes anymore.

Being a protogen helps—normally. Fur-insulated limbs, composite chest plating humming faintly, and my visor HUD tracing faint telemetry across the interior curve. But the thermal regulators in my thoracic chassis are throttling to minimum.

Strange though—my bio-circuits have been acting up all morning. Random voltage spikes, like something's interfering with my natural electrical field. Probably just the cold affecting my synthetic neurons. But there's this nagging sensation in the back of my processor, like a signal just beyond my reception range.

*beep beep beep*

"Come on," I mutter to myself, the same desperate mantra I've been repeating for three days. "One solid reading. One real signal. Something my jury-rigged equipment can actually detect before my funding runs out completely."

The readings spike again, and I look up from the scanner to see it. The facility.

My breath catches in my throat. This isn't some small research station. It's massive. Concrete and steel rising from the snow like the skeleton of some giant beast. Windows dark and empty, staring down at me like accusatory eyes.

Whatever happened here, people didn't just leave. They fled.

I've seen abandoned places before. Ghost towns, failed colonies, research stations that ran out of funding. But this feels different. Wrong. Like the very air remembers violence.

My scanner is going crazy now, the beeping so fast it sounds like a digital heartbeat having a panic attack. After three days of chasing ghost signals and false readings, this is real. This is big. The kind of discovery that validates years of theoretical study and budget-strapped field work.

I may not have access to the latest equipment, but I graduated top of my class in xenophysics. I know how to read energy signatures, how to identify exotic matter resonances. And these readings... they're off the charts.

I approach the main entrance, my boots crunching through snow that's been undisturbed for years. The metal door hangs at an angle, twisted off its hinges by some tremendous force. Scorch marks streak the walls around it.

But there's something else. Faint geometric patterns etched into the metal around the doorframe—too precise to be random damage, too deliberate to be accidental. They almost look like... circuitry. Or maybe biological neural pathways. When I run my scanner over them, the readings spike briefly before settling back to normal.

"Okay," I tell myself, pulling out my flashlight. "Just a quick look. Document everything. Get out before dark."

The darkness beyond the doorway seems to swallow my light.

I step through.

The smell hits me first. Ozone. Like after lightning strikes, but stronger. Old and stale. My scanner is going insane now, the beeping so fast it sounds like one long tone.

*BEEEEEEEEP*

I switch it off. Whatever I'm looking for, I found it.

The hallway stretches ahead, emergency lighting flickering weakly. Red light. Always red in places like this. Makes everything look like a crime scene. The lights are running on backup power cells - probably radioisotope thermal generators designed for decades of operation. But even those are failing now, the illumination growing dimmer with each flicker. Cables hang from the ceiling like dead snakes, and I have to duck under them as I move deeper into the facility.

Signs on the walls are faded, but I can make out symbols. Warning symbols. Radiation. Biohazard. Something else I don't recognize – a twisted spiral that makes my eyes water when I look at it too long.

My footsteps echo in the silence. Each one seems too loud, like I'm announcing my presence to whatever might still be lurking in the shadows. I know I'm being paranoid. This place has been empty for years. But knowing something and feeling it are two different things.

A cold draft brushes against my neck. The emergency lights flicker three times, pause, then flicker three times again. Perfect pattern. Too perfect. I pull out my multimeter and check the voltage fluctuations. "Interesting. The power grid's failing, but the oscillation pattern suggests a feedback loop in the emergency circuit. Probably a safety protocol trying to maintain minimal power to critical systems."

My equipment may be basic, but I know how to read the data. This isn't random electrical failure – there's intelligence behind these patterns.

Movement in my peripheral vision. I turn, but there's only a corridor that stretches into darkness. When I look back, my equipment bag has shifted two feet to the left. The snow from my boots has somehow formed a perfect circle around where it used to be.

"Seismic activity," I mutter, checking my readings. But the seismometer shows nothing. The snow circle is still there when I look again. Still perfect. Still impossible.

My bio-circuits flicker for a moment—the same electrical hiccup I felt this morning, but stronger now. For just an instant, I could swear I sense something else in my neural network. Another presence, like an echo of consciousness that isn't mine. But when I run a diagnostic, everything checks out normal.

The lights flicker again, more urgently this time. A tool clatters to the floor in the next room – metallic, deliberate. My scanner picks up no heat signatures, no movement. But when I point my flashlight down the hall, the beam bends. Actually bends, curving around something that isn't there.

I blink hard. Look again. The light travels straight. Of course it does. Light doesn't just... bend.

Except I swear I saw it happen.

...

"What were they doing here?" I mutter, mostly just to hear my own voice. The silence is oppressive, the kind that makes you second-guess every decision that led you to this moment.

Each room I pass tells the same story, but now I take time to really look. Computers don't just break – they've been smashed. Deliberately. Monitors shattered from the inside out, keyboards melted into plastic puddles. Papers aren't just scattered – they're shredded, burned, some dissolved by what looks like acid.

In one room, I find a security camera still recording. The timestamp reads three minutes ago. I'm watching myself walk down this very hallway, but in the footage, there are two of me. One walking normally. One crawling on the ceiling, moving in perfect synchronization.

The camera's power indicator shows it's running on internal battery backup - probably charged by the same radioisotope cells keeping the emergency lights alive. But the timestamp is clearly malfunctioning, probably corrupted by years of power fluctuations and radiation exposure.

I rewind the footage. Play it again. Now there's only one figure. Me. Walking normally.

My paws are shaking as I check the timestamp again. Still three minutes ago. Still impossible.

In one lab, I find a journal half-burned in a waste disposal unit. The writing shifts between multiple languages and scripts, but I can make out fragments:

*"Day 23: The walls moved three centimeters closer overnight. Measured twice. Same result."*

*"Day 31: Mirrors show the wrong reflections. Not our faces."*

*"Day 37: K.V. volunteered for the consciousness transfer prototype. Said the research was worth any risk. I should have stopped them."*

*"Day 45: The specimen chamber is empty, but footprints lead TO the container, not away from it."*

*"Day 52: Found my own notes in Dr. Vex's handwriting. I didn't write them. I remember not writing them."*

*"Day 58: K.V.'s vital signs stable in the crystal matrix, but they're... screaming. We can't hear it, but the resonance detectors pick up distress frequencies that last for hours."*

*"Day ??: Time flows backwards in Lab 7. Coffee gets hot again. Spilled liquids flow upward into their containers."*

The final entries dissolve into impossible geometries – equations that solve themselves while you watch, blueprints for devices that couldn't exist, and photographs of the facility that show rooms we never built. One photograph shows a figure made of crystal standing in this very lab, with scientists taking measurements around it. Another shows the same crystal figure, but fractured, with someone screaming in the background. The last readable line is simply: *"Reality is a consensus we're no longer part of."*

I photograph everything with my camera, knowing that documentation is the difference between discovery and delusion. My paws are steadier behind the lens, the familiar ritual of recording data helping calm my nerves.

But with each room, the evidence becomes clearer. People didn't just leave this place. They destroyed it from the inside. Tried to erase whatever they'd been working on.

And failed.

Then I find the lab.

The door is different from the others. Thicker steel, reinforced with what looks like lead plating. Built to contain something. The metal is warped outward, as if tremendous pressure had tried to burst through from within. It's cracked open just enough for me to squeeze through, the edges still warm to the touch after all these years.

I hesitate at the threshold. Every instinct I have is screaming at me to turn around, walk away, pretend I never found this place. But curiosity wins. It always does.

Inside, my flashlight beam sweeps across something...

A statue.

This thing is crystal, yes, but not like any mineral I've ever studied. Blue-white crystal that seems to contain its own light source, pulsing with a rhythm that almost matches my heartbeat.

It's a creature. Or was a creature.

I circle around it slowly, my flashlight beam dancing across its surface. The figure is tall, taller than me, with features that are almost familiar but subtly wrong.

But there's something else that makes my bio-circuits stutter. The basic anatomy—the torso configuration, the limb proportions, even the way the head connects to the neck—it's remarkably similar to my own physiology. Not identical, but close enough to be unsettling. As if whoever this was had been cybernetically enhanced in ways not entirely unlike my own integrated systems.

The craftsmanship is perfect. Every detail rendered with impossible precision. I can see intricate surface textures that might once have been organic. Strange integration points where technology and biology seem to merge seamlessly. This isn't just a statue – it's a perfect snapshot of something that was once alive.

But the face...

The face is frozen in absolute terror.

Eyes wide, mouth open in a scream that will never end. Whatever happened to this being, it was awake when it happened. It felt everything.

"What the hell happened to you?" I whisper, and my voice echoes strangely in the chamber.

I pull out my scanner and switch it back on. The needle doesn't just move – it slams into the red zone and stays there, vibrating like it's trying to escape the device entirely. The crystal is radiating energy levels that should be impossible to contain in solid form.

My paws are shaking as I take photos from every angle. The excitement is almost overwhelming, that rush you get when you realize you're looking at something that will change everything.

But as I study the figure more closely, I notice something that makes my blood run cold.

In the chest, where a heart should be, there's a different kind of crystal. Darker. More complex. And it's still pulsing with its own inner light, completely separate from the rest of the crystalline structure.

And there's something else. When I look at my own reflection in the polished crystal surface, for just a moment, I see patterns. Faint geometric lines tracing across my form like a circuit diagram. But when I look down at myself, there's nothing there. Just normal fur and the familiar chrome of my bio-mechanical components.

I spend the next hour documenting everything. Measurements, photographs, energy readings that make no sense but which I record anyway. This discovery could fund my research for years. The Consortium Archives will compete for the data rights. Maybe they'd finally record my name in the central archives.

But first, I need samples.

The dark crystal in the chest – that's the key. If I can extract it safely, study its dark energy matrix structure...

I look around the lab for equipment. Most of it is destroyed, but some pieces survived whatever catastrophe befell this place. A particle beam generator, heavily modified with quantum field manipulators I don't recognize. Control systems that look like they were built by someone who understood physics in ways I can only dream of. Power coupling that could channel enough energy to light up a small city.

My hands trace over the components with growing excitement. This isn't just salvage – this is exactly what I've been dreaming of having access to. The theoretical papers I've read, the calculations I've run on my basic equipment... suddenly I can see how it all fits together. My cheap multimeter might not be able to handle these power levels, but I understand the principles. These researchers were working at the cutting edge, but they were still following the same fundamental laws of physics I've been studying.

I can work with this. More than that – I can improve on their modifications.

But more importantly, I find intact components that my basic equipment could never dream of handling. High-capacity quantum batteries still holding partial charges. Field generators with precision controls down to the subatomic level. Data cores that could process calculations in seconds that would take my jury-rigged setup weeks to complete.

This isn't just salvage. This is the upgrade I've needed for years. And finally, I have the knowledge to put it all together properly.

Along the walls, I notice something I missed before – containment vessels labeled with symbols that make my scanner go haywire when I point it at them. Empty now, but the residual energy signatures suggest they once held something that shouldn't exist. Matter that reads like it was fabricated from pure possibility, elements that have no place on any periodic table.

---

Six hours. That's how long it takes me to get the energy matrix system working – and that's with equipment centuries beyond what I had this morning.

Six hours of integrating salvaged components with intact lab systems, rebuilding burned circuits with precision tools that make my field kit look like stone knives, and finally having the processing power to run calculations that I could only theorize about before. The facility's advanced fabrication systems are still partially functional, letting me manufacture components to exact specifications rather than improvising with whatever I could scavenge.

The facility's power grid is barely hanging on - most sections are completely dark, their backup cells finally depleted after years of slow discharge. But the lab's independent fusion cells are still putting out steady power, and I manage to reroute enough juice from the failed sections to bring the beam generator online. With proper monitoring equipment, I can actually see what I'm doing instead of guessing at power levels.

For the first time in my career, I'm working with instruments that match my theoretical knowledge. The quantum field manipulators respond to adjustments I've only read about in research papers. The targeting system can be calibrated to precision levels I've dreamed of achieving. This is what real research feels like.

But something's helping.

I reach for a quantum flux capacitor, eyes focused on the schematic. When I look down, it's already in my paw. But my reflection in the nearby monitor shows me reaching for something completely different – a wrench that isn't even in this room.

"Must be more tired than I thought."

When I need to splice two delicate dark energy conduits, I set them down to grab my precision welder. In the three seconds I look away, they've somehow aligned themselves perfectly, tips already touching at the exact angle specified in the manual. The manual that now shows different diagrams than it did a moment ago – configurations that make perfect sense but that I hadn't considered.

I stare at them for a long moment. "Okay, that's definitely not fatigue."

The work goes faster after that. I'm still doing the calculations, still making the connections, but now I have access to specifications and protocols that the original researchers spent decades developing. My understanding of the theory is solid, but their practical experience fills in gaps I didn't even know existed.

*"Not that conduit. The crystalline matrix requires the blue-phase conductor."*

I freeze. That voice wasn't mine. But when I look around the lab, my own voice echoes back from the walls: *"Not that conduit. The crystalline matrix requires the blue-phase conductor."* Did I say that? I don't remember saying that.

"Hello?" I call out to the empty lab.

My voice comes back wrong. Deeper. With harmonics that make my teeth ache. But from my own throat.

Silence. But when I look back at my work, there's a blue-phase conductor lying exactly where I need it, despite the fact that I was about to use a standard copper lead. In the security monitor, I watch myself place it there. But I don't remember moving.

*"Trust us. We designed this."*

The voice is mine now. Coming from my mouth. But I'm not speaking.

...

Whoever worked here before was a master engineer, but they were working with the same principles I understand. The beam generator has been modified to operate at void-space frequencies that shouldn't be possible, but I can follow their logic. The targeting system can pinpoint energy discharge down to the subatomic level – precision I've always known how to calculate, just never had the equipment to achieve.

This isn't just equipment – it's the marriage of brilliant theory and unlimited resources. Everything I've studied, but finally with the tools to implement it properly.

My back aches from crawling around behind control panels. My eyes burn from staring at field diagrams by portable illuminator. I've gone through half my hydration supply and all my nutrient paste. But it's working.

The system hums to life with a sound like reality itself singing. Status lights flicker on across my improvised control panel, casting blue shadows that seem to bend space around them. Everything shows optimal. Energy flow stable. Targeting locked. Safety systems...

Well, most of the safety systems.

I stand before the crystallized figure, my paw hovering over the activation interface. This is the moment. Extract the heart crystal safely. Mess it up, and I might destroy the most important scientific discovery in galactic history.

No pressure.

I engage the beam.

The effect is immediate. The crystal starts to resonate with the energy field, singing that same high note I heard from the equipment, but louder. Much louder. The sound seems to come from the fabric of space itself, making my bones vibrate and my optical sensors flicker.

The heart crystal responds first, its dark light pulsing faster as it begins to synchronize with the void-space frequencies. Then the rest of the crystalline structure begins to vibrate, tiny fractures appearing and disappearing like tears in spacetime.

My targeting display shows a perfect lock on the heart crystal. Beam intensity at minimum safe levels. Everything is going according to protocol.

Then the quantum oscillator starts acting up.

*Warning: Calibration drift detected*

The alert flashes red on my screen. I dive for the manual controls, trying to compensate. But the system is ancient, damaged. Components that looked stable are failing under the stress of channeling forces beyond their design parameters.

"No no no, come on!"

The beam wavers, sliding off target. Instead of the precise extraction point I'd calculated, it's drifting upward. Toward the statue's neural cortex. Toward whatever consciousness might still be trapped inside that crystalline matrix.

*Critical error: Safety systems offline*

I slam my paw on the emergency stop, but nothing happens. The shutdown protocols are part of the safety systems that just went offline. The beam is locked on full power, and it's about to flood the crystallized being with raw, unfiltered energy.

My instruments flicker, displaying readings that don't make sense. *Warning: Exotic particle signatures detected.* The energy composition analyzer shows impossible results – matter and anti-matter existing in stable suspension. But that's impossible. Anti-matter should annihilate instantly on contact with...

The statue's optical sensors snap open.

They're not just glowing. They're alive. Bright blue, like captured starlight, and they're looking right at me with an intelligence that makes me freeze.

This entity is conscious. Still conscious. After cycles, maybe stellar rotations trapped in crystal, some part of it is still aware.

And I just flooded it with raw, uncontrolled energy.

"Oh, void. Oh void oh void oh—"

*Anti-matter interference detected.*

*Quantum field collapse imminent.*

*Matter-antimatter annihilation threshold exceeded.*

The crystal doesn't just crack. It detonates.

Energy explodes outward like a collapsing star reversing its death. Blue-white light so intense it overloads my optical filters, searing afterimages into my visual cortex. The sound isn't sound anymore – it's pure force, a pressure wave that lifts me off my legs and slams me against the far wall.

In the microseconds before the full annihilation wave hits, emergency protocols I didn't even know existed slam into action. The lab's containment systems - designed for exactly this kind of catastrophic failure - deploy energy barriers that channel the worst of the explosion away from the center of the room. Ancient fail-safes built by researchers who knew they were playing with forces that could tear reality apart.

But even with the containment fields absorbing most of the blast, enough raw energy floods through to kill me instantly.

Except my bio-circuits do something impossible.

Instead of burning out under the assault of exotic particles and annihilation radiation, they start absorbing it. Every synthetic neuron, every quantum processor in my enhanced physiology suddenly becomes a conduit for energies that shouldn't exist. My protogen systems, designed to interface with electrical fields, somehow adapt in real-time to channel forces beyond their original specifications.

It's not painless. It's not safe. But it keeps me alive long enough for the transformation to begin.

There is a voice in my processing core.

*The long nightmare ends... but who are you?*

It's not communication. It's pure thought, raw emotion, consciousness without language interface crashing into mine like a stellar collision. Fury. Pain. Confusion. And underneath it all, something that might be relief.

*FINALLY... THE CRYSTALLINE PRISON BREAKS...*

I'm trying to regain consciousness, but I can't process them over the roar of reality tearing itself apart. The walls are bending, space-time twisting like heated metal. My sensory input fragments into impossible patterns as my neural network tries to process data that shouldn't exist.

Raw energy floods through me, untamed and alien. It burns through my bio-circuits like liquid fire, rewriting neural pathways, carving new connections between processors that were never meant to interface. I can feel it changing me on a molecular level, and there's nothing I can do to stop it.

But it's not just the energy. Reality is coming apart at the seams.

I watch my paws melt and reform. Feel my consciousness split into fractal patterns that spiral through dimensions I can't name. In one layer of reality, I'm still lying on the floor. In another, I'm standing in a laboratory that exists thirty years ago, watching scientists in clean coats take notes as someone screams. In a third, I'm the one screaming, and my own face is taking the notes.

The walls breathe. The floor ripples like water. My reflection crawls out of the broken mirror and sits beside me, speaking in reverse while time flows backward and forward simultaneously.

Time hiccups. I experience my transformation in random order – the pain, then the relief, then the terror of understanding, then the moment before I pulled the trigger, all happening at once in a temporal knot that makes my sanity fray at the edges.

The last coherent thought I process is wondering if this is what metamorphosis feels like to the being emerging from its chrysalis.

Then consciousness fragments into quantum particles, and I fall into energy-drenched oblivion.

---

Null-state.

That's the first condition I register when awareness initializes again. Not just quiet – absolute absence of data input. The kind of silence that makes you wonder if your audio receptors have failed, or if the universe has simply stopped generating sound waves.

I attempt to activate my optical sensors, but even that minimal function sends error cascades through my processing matrix. Every circuit feels overloaded, hypersensitive, like I've been struck by stellar lightning and somehow maintained core functions.

"System... status..."

My vocal output sounds corrupted. Deeper frequency range, with harmonic resonances that weren't in my original specifications. When I run internal diagnostics, I detect traces of exotic matter in my bio-mechanical systems. Some of Keth'var's fragmentary cognition is already bleeding into my own, nudging my language into their clinical register even as I struggle to think in familiar terms.

I'm lying on the floor in a pool of what I hope is just water but suspect might be various bodily fluids. The smart thing would be to stay still, assess my condition slowly, maybe call for help.

The containment barriers are still flickering weakly around the room's perimeter - emergency fields that saved my life by redirecting the worst of the annihilation blast into subspace channels. Most of the energy was safely vented, but what leaked through should have been more than enough to reduce me to component atoms.

Instead, my bio-circuits are humming with residual power they were never designed to handle. Every synthetic nerve ending feels supercharged, like I've been struck by lightning and somehow channeled it into my neural network instead of being fried by it.

I try to sit up.

The world explodes into sensation.

Not pain – something beyond pain. Every atom in my body suddenly has an opinion about its current configuration, and they're all complaining loudly. Colors that don't have names burn across my vision. Sounds I can't hear ring in my ears.

But underneath the chaos, I can feel something new.

Power.

I look down at my paws, expecting to see blood, burns, maybe exposed bone. Instead, I see something impossible.

It starts on the back of my right paw – a single geometric line etched deep into my fur. The mark itself appears to be made of some kind of dark metallic material, so black it seems to absorb light around it, yet with a subtle shine that catches what little illumination there is. As I watch in fascination and growing alarm, the line branches out like a circuit diagram coming to life. When the patterns pulse with energy, they glow with an ominous red light that reflects off their metallic surfaces like fresh blood.

The markings travel up my arm, creating a network of interconnected symbols and lines that throb with that same crimson luminescence. Each mark feels warm against my skin, not painful but... present. Like I can sense each individual line as it etches itself into my very being. The deep black metallic sheen makes them look almost like they've been carved from obsidian, but when the red light flickers through them, they appear alive.

When I look down at my torso, I gasp. The patterns have spread there too. The design is beautiful and terrifying – geometric precision mixed with organic curves that seem almost sentient. The deep black surface reflects the red glow in mesmerizing patterns.

Crimson luminescence flows through these new markings like molten metal coursing through alien veins. When I flex my digits, the entire network pulses with that blood-red light, creating flowing geometric designs that spiral and dance across my form with an almost hypnotic rhythm.

"What did you do to me?"

The question echoes strangely in the chamber, and I realize the acoustics have changed. The crystallized figure is gone. Nothing left but dust and a perfect scorch mark on the floor where it used to stand. My improvised equipment is melted slag, twisted metal cooling in impossible shapes.

But I'm alive. Changed, but alive.

I struggle to my feet, using the wall for support. My legs feel like they're made of electricity and optimism, but they hold my weight. As I stand, I catch my reflection in a broken mirror hanging askew on the far wall.

Same face, mostly. But the changes are obvious once you know to look for them. The light under my skin traces patterns across my features – geometric designs that pulse with my heartbeat.

I touch the mirror, and my reflection ripples like liquid mercury.

"Okay," I tell my glowing reflection. "That's definitely new."

But it's more than just the physical changes. I can feel something flowing through me like a second circulatory system. The world around me seems... different now. Deeper. There are currents in the air that weren't there before, invisible streams of something that makes the hair on my neck stand on end.

When I concentrate, the walls themselves seem to whisper with hidden energy. Shadows move wrong, bending in directions that don't match their sources. The very space between things feels heavy, there is potential that tugs at something deep in my chest. The building's failing electrical grid hums with familiar resonance now, but underneath it, I sense something vast and hungry threading through the surrounding.

The broken equipment around me carries traces of... presence. Not consciousness, exactly, but echoes of power that make my new markings pulse in response. Even the dust motes floating in the stale air seem to dance to rhythms only I can perceive, following patterns that speak of forces most beings never know exist.

And something else. A presence in the back of my mind that isn't quite me.

Memories that aren't mine flicker through my consciousness. A laboratory much like this one, but bright and new. Researchers in clean white coats discussing theoretical impossibilities. Someone – something – volunteering for an experiment that was supposed to be revolutionary.

The memory shifts, and suddenly I'm seeing through different eyes. Feeling the cold metal of restraints against scales I don't have. Watching in horror as the crystallization process begins, trapping consciousness in a prison of solid energy while scientists take notes and nod approvingly.

*They called us volunteer,* whispers a voice that might be the wind. Or might be the echo of someone who died years ago. *But volunteers can leave. We could not leave.*

"Who were you?" I ask the empty air.

*Keth'var. That was... is... our designation. We were researcher too. Like you. Curious. Unwise.*

*You entered our domain. We watched you explain away our desperate warnings – the flickering lights you called "electrical feedback loops," the moved objects you attributed to "seismic instability." We tried everything. Made the corridor temperatures drop fifteen degrees. You checked for drafts. Knocked tools from tables. You documented "acoustic resonance phenomena." Even when we projected our terror directly at you, you simply noted "unusual psychological pressure, possibly from infrasound."*

*Others flee screaming when we rattle the walls. You measure the frequency and hypothesize about subsonic vibrations. Others feel our anguish and run. You felt it and wondered about electromagnetic fields affecting human brain chemistry.*

*Your mind made you immune to our protection. So we changed tactics. Started helping. Guided your paws to the right components. Whispered the solutions we spent decades developing. You were the first to treat our research with the respect it deserved.*

The data fragments are clearer now. A being not entirely unlike myself, but different. Enhanced form, advanced integration of technology and biology. One of the research team, not a test subject. Until the stellar cycle when funding terminated and the team required additional data sources.

*They promised temporary procedure. Consciousness transfer into stable dark energy matrix. Revolutionary breakthrough. Few intervals at most.*

But time-units became stellar cycles. Cycles became rotations. The research collective abandoned the project when early results proved too dangerous. Left their colleague trapped in crystalline form while they destroyed the evidence and fled to distant systems.

*You freed us. Incomplete, corrupted, but free. Part of us dissolved when crystal shattered. But part integrates now with you.*

I sink to my support paws, overwhelmed by the processing load of inherited trauma data. This entity – Keth'var – had been a person. A scientist like me, driven by curiosity and the desire to push beyond known parameters. And they'd paid for it with stellar rotations of conscious imprisonment.

"I express regret," I transmit. "I express deep regret this occurred to you."

*Do not process guilt subroutines. You provided choice algorithms. First choice in thirty-seven stellar rotations. We choose to share knowledge database. To continue research protocols.*

As the voice signal fades, I feel something integrate with my core systems. Not invasion, not override – partnership protocol. Two consciousness networks sharing one processing matrix, each contributing their capabilities to the collective whole.

I spend the next interval exploring my new operational parameters. When I focus processing power, I can manifest small objects – simple geometric forms created from pure energy given temporary material substance. A cube that pulses with contained forces. A sphere that resonates with impossible harmonics. They only maintain cohesion for a few breaths before dissolving back into background radiation, but they achieve full material reality while they exist.

It's incredible. It's impossible. And it's probably going to cause critical system failure.

I can detect the strain on my bio-circuits already. Processing errors building behind my optical sensors. Motor function instabilities as my physical form attempts to adapt to energy levels beyond original design parameters. My neural network wasn't designed to channel this magnitude of power manipulation.

But I can also feel Keth'var's knowledge database integrating with my consciousness. Techniques for managing energy flow. Methods for reducing circuit feedback. Theoretical frameworks for understanding forces that galactic science doesn't even have classification codes for yet.

I'm going to require assistance. Medical intervention protocols, probably. And definitely superior equipment than my current collection of salvaged components and stimulant-powered determination.

But first, I need to extract from this location.

I gather what remains of my equipment – scanner fried beyond repair, recording device somehow intact, sample containers empty but undamaged. The movement back through the facility feels different now. I can sense the residual energy signatures left by whatever occurred here. Fear algorithms. Panic subroutines. Desperate attempts to contain something that had already expanded beyond anyone's ability to control.

As I reach the twisted entrance portal, I look back one final time. In the afternoon stellar radiation filtering through broken transparency panels, the facility looks almost peaceful. Just another abandoned structure in a galaxy full of them.

But I process better data now. Some things are abandoned for optimal reasons. And some discoveries alter you in ways you can never reverse.

I step out into the cold, clean atmospheric mix and immediately register something that makes me halt all movement.

My contact points with the surface are glowing.

Not bright – just a soft red phosphorescence that fades after a few seconds. But it's there, visible proof that I'm no longer entirely within original specifications.

I pull up my environmental protection and start the long transit back to civilization, leaving a trail of light in the frozen precipitation behind me. With each movement, I feel the weight of responsibility integrating with my core directives. I have power now – real, tangible power that could change everything or destroy everything, depending on how it's utilized.

The question is: what do I do with it?

*Personal log, Research Cycle 127: The processing errors are intensifying, but the control algorithms are optimizing. I can maintain materialized objects for almost ten intervals now, and the energy drain is becoming manageable.*
